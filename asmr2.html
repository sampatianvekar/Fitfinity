What is ASMR

ASMR, which stands for Autonomous Sensory Meridian Response, is a pleasant tingling sensation that some individuals experience in response to specific auditory, visual, or tactile triggers. ASMR has gained popularity in recent years as a method for relaxation, stress reduction, and improved sleep. The sensation typically begins on the scalp and travels down the back of the neck and spine, often accompanied by a feeling of deep relaxation and well-being. Researcher Dr. Giulia PoerioTrusted Source explains that the following audiovisual stimuli (also known as triggers) may produce ASMR:

  • whispering or speaking softly
  • tapping, scratching, or crinkling sounds
  • slow hand movements
  • repetitive tasks

Benefits of ASMR

  • Improved mood. An early study revealed that 80 percentTrusted Source of participants had improved mood after exposure to ASMR. The benefits declined faster in people with higher levels of depression.
  • Pain relief. The same studyTrusted Source showed that a portion of people who deal with chronic pain found relief that lasted for up to 3 hours after exposure.
  • Deeper concentration. A 'flow state' refers to when you lose yourself or your sense of time because you are so deeply concentrated on something. ResearchersTrusted Source have found similarities between ASMR and the type of focus you could experience in flow.
  • Better sleep. Early research has shown that ASMR may help get you and your brain into the right state for deep, healthy sleep by promoting relaxation and theta brainwaves.
  • Other benefits. People who enjoy ASMR often report reduced anxiety, fewer headaches, lower blood pressure, and more. While these are in line with what has been proven, more research is needed to back up these claims.
